How it works

The arm has two straight parts: an upper arm (12 cm) and a forearm (9 cm). You choose how much each joint turns, and the tool works out where the tip ends up. Going from angles to a position like this is called forward kinematics.

θ1 is measured from the floor. θ2 is measured from the line of the upper arm, so it adds on top of θ1. That's why the forearm's direction is θ1 + θ2 in the formulas above.

Here you do the reverse: you pick where the tip should go, and the tool works out the joint angles. Going from a position to angles is called inverse kinematics.

The base, the elbow and the tip form a triangle with known side lengths. A triangle rule called the law of cosines gives the elbow angle, and from that the shoulder angle follows. There are usually two answers (elbow up or elbow down); the tool picks elbow up when it can.
